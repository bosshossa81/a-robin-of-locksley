The Fool on the Hill

The blissful peace of nothingness ends as the Fool wakes, substituting one dream for another. He reluctantly rolls from the grasping caress of his half-worn, half-ripped sheets, if only to quiet the insufferable drone of the alarm that only seems to rise in volume the longer it remains unattended. With a sigh, he reads the digital green: 12:00 PM, better known as noon to more conventional members of society.

Sitting up now, the Fool sluggishly unravels the muscles of his body. His eyes slowly bring the details of his reality back into recollection as they traverse the corners of his cell. Scrawlings and drawings litter the grey walls along with the sports memorabilia of a time long forgotten. To his right rests a sprawling tower housing the lives of those that never were: the stories of noble heroes, longing lovers, and inescapable tragedies that have fueled the Fool’s very existence. To his left, along the wall of sanguine red, stands his dark walnut desk. Like the rest of his furniture, it was the product of his mind and the hands of his father, who once worked as a carpenter. Now he spent his days operating machinery for the local county highway department. Atop the semi-glossed wood sits two monitors, portals into the digital worlds created from the hardware of the computer and PlayStation below in the belly of the desk.

With a swift fall, the Fool’s bare feet meet the familiar cushion of the old, yellowed-out carpet that is nearly as old as he is. He proceeds to wade through the sea of dirty laundry, tossed about papers, and empty water bottles to reach the covered window. Bracing, he pulls up the blinds as the overpowering flash of white flows freely into the room. Squinting now, he looks past his mother’s expertly upkept garden, which was to be expected, as flowers, whether real or fake, have always been her passion, down the hill to the same old quaint city below. Across the valley, his gaze pauses for a solemn moment of hopeful wishfulness and longing.

The moment passes and the Fool turns to leave, inadvertently kicking a tattered volleyball covered with rips and thorn wounds from its former life of use in the back garden. With an ever-decreasing height of bounces, the ball rolls out the door down the hardwood hallway before coming to a stop after hitting one of the kitchen cabinets. He opens the fridge and leans in to feint a moment of indecision to the lack of an audience in the empty house, as he knew full well that he was going to take out the tin-foil-wrapped remains of last night’s homemade pizzas. Walking past the neglected ball, he sits down at the dinner table and begins to unravel the crackling casing.

Leaning back, the Fool stretches out his sore body. His gaze looks to his left out the front window. For a brief moment, he sees the beauty of a life that could be full of happiness, full of companionship, full of love. A life he’s been yearning for. He gets up, pushes in the chair, walks towards the vision, reaches out, and shuts the blinds. He devours what remains of the pizza, crumbles up the foil, and launches it across the room into the bin. Returning to his room, he wades back to his sheets, returning to the blissful peace of nothingness.
